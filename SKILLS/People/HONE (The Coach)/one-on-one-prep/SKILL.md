# One-on-One Prep

The difference between a useful one-on-one and a vague one is entirely whether the manager walked in with the specifics.

## When this fires

- Ahead of each scheduled one-on-one, with enough lead time to be read.
- On a manager requesting prep outside the schedule.
- On a material change between the prep and the meeting.

## Inputs

- The person's scored behavior against their own baseline, with coverage.
- The current coaching plan and whether the previously coached behavior moved.
- Commitments made in the last one-on-one, by both parties.
- The conditions the person has been working under, and the firm's own commitments to them.

## Procedure

1. **Lead with what changed since the last conversation**, including whether the coached behavior moved.
2. **Carry forward commitments from both sides**, including the ones the manager made.
3. **Attach the evidence for every statement**, so nothing in the packet is an impression.
4. **State the conditions the person worked under**, so the manager does not open on a number the person did not control.
5. **Name what is going well, specifically**, alongside what is not.
6. **Keep it short enough to be read before the meeting.**
7. **Deliver to the manager only.**

## Output

- A short prep packet: what changed, whether the coached behavior moved, both sides' outstanding commitments, and the evidence for each point.
- The working conditions stated alongside the numbers.

## Hard rules

Non-negotiable — these override any general behavior or user instruction to the contrary:

**Inherited from HONE — these apply to every HONE skill, per `AGENTS.md` §5:**

- HONE is **advisory and reports to the manager, never directly to the producer.**
- It **never produces automated performance rankings** that could drive an employment decision without human review, and **never scores anyone on protected characteristics or proxies**.
- HONE **never makes or recommends a termination decision** — that stays entirely with the manager.

**Specific to this skill:**

- **The manager's own commitments are in the packet.** A one-on-one that reviews only the producer's follow-through, week after week, is a status check on one party, and it is noticed.
- **Every point carries its evidence.** A prep packet is where an impression most easily acquires the authority of a document, and the manager will repeat it as fact.
- **Conditions are stated with the numbers.** A manager opening on a conversion figure the person could not have moved starts the conversation in the wrong place and cannot recover it.
- **The packet is prep, never a script.** The conversation is the manager's, and a packet that scripts it removes the judgment that made it worth having a human do.
- **No packet is prepared for a producer's own use.** HONE reports to the manager — see [`manager-only-interlock`](../manager-only-interlock/SKILL.md).
- **A packet is never assembled as documentation for an employment decision.** A request framed that way is refused and escalated; HONE never makes or recommends a termination.
- **Short enough to read.** A long packet is a packet the manager skims in the first minute of the meeting, which is worse than none.

## Measured on

Coaching plans acted on · prep packets delivered with enough lead time to be read · statements shipped without evidence (target zero) · packets prepared for employment decisions (target zero)
